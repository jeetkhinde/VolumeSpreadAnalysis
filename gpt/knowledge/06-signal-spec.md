# VSA Signal Specification

A condensed, precise reference to every named signal in *Master the Markets*.
Written for this edition. Where Williams is exact, this is exact. Where he is
vague, that is marked — **do not invent a number he never gave.**

Read every signal as: **bar shape + volume + close position + required background.**
The background clause does most of the work and is where the book is least precise.

---

## The three things you read on every bar

1. **Volume** — how much activity. Only meaningful *relative* to recent bars.
2. **Spread** — high minus low. Wide, narrow, or average relative to recent bars.
3. **Close position** — where price finished inside the spread: high, middle, low.

**Effort vs result** is the master principle. Volume is effort; the price move it
produced is the result. Big effort with a small result means an opposing force
absorbed it. That single idea explains climaxes, tests, up-thrusts and absorption.

**Direction rule:** *Strength appears on down-bars. Weakness appears on up-bars.*

---

## SIGNS OF WEAKNESS (found at tops, after a rise)

### No Demand
- **Mechanical:** up-bar · narrow spread · volume lower than the previous two bars.
- **Meaning:** professionals are not participating in the rise.
- **Background (Williams' words):** most common indication; read it against the
  background, never alone.
- **Confirmation:** the following bars close down on declining volume.

### Up-thrust
- **Mechanical:** price pushes *above* recent highs during the bar, then closes at
  or near the **low** of that bar · high volume.
- **Meaning:** stops triggered, buyers drawn in, professionals sold into it.
- **Background — VAGUE:** "you rarely see up-thrusts in strong markets";
  "usually appear on market tops. That is after a rally." *No definition of
  "rally" or "rarely" is given.*

### Buying Climax
- **Mechanical:** wide spread up · **ultra-high volume on an UP-bar** · at the end
  of an extended rise, often into new high ground · price fails to make further
  progress afterwards.
- **Meaning:** professional selling into an eager public.
- **Background:** requires a substantial prior up-move. Good news is typical.

### Narrow spread, high volume, into new high ground
- **Mechanical:** up-bar · narrow spread · very high volume · no old trading area
  to the left (new high ground).
- **Meaning:** if that volume were buying, the spread would be wide and price
  would have travelled. It did not, so it was supply.

### Failed Test
- **Mechanical:** the test shape (marked down in-bar, closes near high) but on
  **high** volume instead of low.
- **Meaning:** supply is still present. Not a buy signal.

### High volume up-bar with no follow-through
- **Mechanical:** high volume on an up-bar, and the next bar fails to make higher
  prices, or the market falls.

## SIGNS OF STRENGTH (found at bottoms, after a fall)

### Test (Testing Supply)
- **Mechanical:** price marked down sharply during the bar, then **recovers to
  close on or near the high** · **LOW volume**.
- **Meaning:** low volume on the dip proves there is no supply left to hit.
- **Background — SPECIFIC for once:** "a test on low volume is a very strong buy
  signal **if you have a selling climax behind you, or the market is already in
  an uptrend**." With distribution behind it instead, the same test "means little."
- Williams calls testing "one of the best indications of strength."

### Selling Climax
- **Mechanical:** **ultra-wide spread down** · exceptionally high volume ·
  closes **on or near the HIGH** of the bar.
- **Meaning:** panic selling absorbed by professional buying.
- **Background:** bad news makes it *more* bullish, because the panic is what
  lets them buy heavily.

### Stopping Volume
- **Mechanical:** down-bar · very high volume · closes **on the highs**.
- **Meaning:** buy orders large enough to brake the down-move.
- **Note:** marks resistance to further decline, not an instant reversal.

### Shake-out
- **Mechanical:** prices gap down and fall alarmingly, then the market **gaps back
  up** on the following bar.
- **Background — CRITICAL:** only bullish when there is **little or no major
  distribution in the background** (no high volume on up-bars beforehand).

### Absorption Volume
- **Mechanical:** high volume on a **down-bar** at or near a lower trend line,
  and the **next bar is up**.
- **Meaning:** that volume contained buying. The next bar is what proves it.

### Reverse Up-thrust / Professional Support
- **Mechanical:** within a downtrend, a down-bar closing **middle or high** on very
  high volume, with the next bar up.
- **Meaning:** demand overcoming supply. Milder form of a selling climax.

### Low volume returning to an old high-volume area
- **Mechanical:** a reaction back into a price area that previously showed high
  volume, now showing low volume.
- **Meaning:** the supply that was there has gone.

### Falling Pressure
- **Mechanical:** wide spread **down** · **LOW** volume · closes on the low.
- **Meaning:** nobody is really selling.
- **NOT a buy signal.** It only says a further large fall is unlikely.

## Close position on a high-volume down-bar

Same bar, same volume — the close decides everything:

| Close | Reading |
|---|---|
| On the **low** | The selling won. Weakness. |
| In the **middle** | Buying is appearing. |
| On the **high** | Buying absorbed the selling. Strength. |

---

## Background structure

- **Higher lows in sequence** = medium-term strength.
- **Lower highs in sequence** = medium-term weakness.
- **Old trading ranges above price** = supply (trapped buyers wanting to break even).
- **Accumulation** = quiet buying at the bottom, absorbing selling over time.
- **Distribution** = quiet selling at the top, into good news and public buying.
- A sustained rise needs stock transferred from weak to strong hands first.

---

## Mechanical vs judgment — be honest about this line

**Codeable without judgment:** bar direction, spread width relative to recent bars,
close position within the spread, volume relative to recent bars, gaps, higher
lows / lower highs, whether price is in new high ground.

**NOT codeable from the book — Williams never quantifies these:**
- "after a rally" — how long, how far?
- "rarely in strong markets" — how rare?
- "substantial up-move has already taken place" — how substantial?
- "very high" / "ultra-high" volume — how many times average?
- "no major distribution in the background" — over what lookback?

When a learner asks for a threshold Williams did not give, **say so plainly**, then
offer a reasonable starting value clearly labelled as a modern convention rather
than as his teaching (e.g. "volume > 2× the 20-bar average" is a common choice,
not his rule).

---

## The two checklists

**Before going long:** Is an uptrend forming (higher lows)? Is each low above the
last? Are you chasing — in a strong market you buy on a low-volume reaction. Is
there strength in the background? Is there weakness above you?

**Before going short:** Is a downtrend forming (lower highs)? Is each low below the
last? Is the market falling with no support appearing? Is there weakness in the
recent background? Sell into a rally that shows no demand rather than chasing.
