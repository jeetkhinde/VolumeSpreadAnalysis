Just as with bullish patterns, bearish candlestick patterns only become high-probability signals when verified by Volume Spread Analysis. 

Below is a guide on how to apply VSA methods to verify 5 well-known bearish candlestick patterns.

### 1. Shooting Star
**Traditional Definition:** A small body with a long upper wick (price pushes up but gets rejected and closes near the low).

**VSA Correlation:** **Up-thrust**.

<Diagram name="candlestick-shooting-star" />

- **Verification:** A true Up-thrust should have **HIGH** volume. It means stops were triggered and the public rushed to buy, allowing professionals to sell heavily into the surge.
- **Background Check:** An Up-thrust is only credible if it happens after a rally (in a strong market) or when hitting an old resistance/supply level.

### 2. Bearish Engulfing
**Traditional Definition:** A bullish candle followed by a bearish candle that engulfs the previous body.

**VSA Correlation:** **Buying Climax** followed by supply.

<Diagram name="candlestick-engulfing-bear" />

- **Verification:** If the down-bar closes on its lows with high volume, it proves supply has overcome demand.
- **Trap Warning:** If the first (bullish) bar had ultra-high volume but failed to progress further on the second bar, it was a Buying Climax.

### 3. Dark Cloud Cover
**Traditional Definition:** A bullish candle followed by a bearish candle that opens higher but closes more than halfway down the previous bullish body.

**VSA Correlation:** **No Result from Effort** or Supply overcoming Demand.

- **Verification:** If the bullish bar had high volume, it showed effort. The fact that the next bar opens higher but closes poorly means the effort yielded no result—someone absorbed the buying and pushed the price down. 

### 4. Tweezer Top
**Traditional Definition:** Two or more consecutive bars with matching highs, indicating strong resistance.

**VSA Correlation:** **No Demand** into an area of previous high volume.

- **Verification:** The first top is often made on high volume (a buying climax). The second top must be made on **LOWER** volume than the first, proving that demand has dried up (a classic No Demand signal) and the path of least resistance is now down.

### 5. Evening Star
**Traditional Definition:** A multi-bar reversal showing a slowdown in upward momentum followed by a sharp push down.

**VSA Correlation:** **No Demand** at a market top.

<Diagram name="candlestick-evening-star" />

- **Verification:** The middle bar is often an up-bar with a narrow spread. If the volume on this bar is **LOWER** than the previous two bars, it is a classic **No Demand** signal.
- **Follow-through:** The third (bearish) bar should confirm the weakness by closing down on increasing volume.

### The Master Rule for All Patterns
As Tom Williams taught, you cannot read a single bar (or candlestick pattern) in isolation. **The background decides everything.**

- Do not buy a Bullish Engulfing pattern if the background is full of distribution (lower highs, high volume on up-bars that fail to progress).
- Do not sell a Shooting Star (Up-thrust) if the background is full of accumulation (higher lows, selling climaxes).
