Traditional candlestick patterns provide a visual summary of price action, but they only tell half the story. As Tom Williams emphasizes, price action without volume is like a car without an engine.

To verify the credibility of any bullish candlestick pattern, we must apply **Volume Spread Analysis (VSA)**—specifically analyzing the effort (volume) versus the result (spread and close), while keeping the background context in mind.

Below is a guide on how to apply VSA methods to verify 5 well-known bullish candlestick patterns. We have rendered the traditional "candlesticks" as standard VSA price bars (High, Low, and Close) to focus on the absolute close position, which is critical in VSA.

### Note on VSA Thresholds
Tom Williams deliberately left certain thresholds vague (like "ultra-high volume" or "after a rally") because volume is relative to the recent background. As a starting point, modern analysts often use moving averages to quantify these:
- **High Volume:** Volume significantly above the 20-bar moving average.
- **Ultra-High Volume:** Volume at least 2x to 3x the 20-bar moving average.
- **Low Volume:** Volume visibly below the average of the previous two bars.

### 1. Hammer
**Traditional Definition:** A small body with a long lower wick (price opens, drops significantly, but closes near the high).

**VSA Correlation:** Depending on volume, this is either a **Selling Climax** or a **Test**.

- **Low Volume:** If volume is low, it is a classic **Test** (Testing Supply). It proves there is no selling pressure left to push the market down. This is a very strong buy signal if the background shows a previous selling climax or an uptrend.
- **Ultra-High Volume:** If volume is ultra-high, it is a **Selling Climax** or Shake-out. The long lower wick means professionals stepped in to absorb panic selling.

### 2. Bullish Engulfing
**Traditional Definition:** A bearish candle followed by a larger bullish candle that engulfs the previous body.

**VSA Correlation:** **Absorption Volume** or Professional Support.

<Diagram name="candlestick-engulfing-bull" />

- **Verification:** The bullish bar must show healthy, rising volume to indicate that professional money is stepping in to absorb supply.
- **Trap Warning:** If the volume on the bullish engulfing bar is *ultra-high* but the very next bar fails to go higher, it might be a disguised trap (professionals selling into the buying frenzy).

### 3. Piercing Pattern
**Traditional Definition:** A bearish candle followed by a bullish candle that opens lower but closes more than halfway up the previous bearish body.

**VSA Correlation:** Early **Professional Support**.

- **Verification:** Similar to the Bullish Engulfing, the volume on the bullish bar should be high to show effort. The close must be strong (near the high of the bar) to show that the effort had a result. 

### 4. Tweezer Bottom
**Traditional Definition:** Two or more consecutive bars with matching lows, indicating strong support.

**VSA Correlation:** A **Test** into an area of previous high volume.

- **Verification:** The first bottom is often made on high volume (a climax or shakeout). The second bottom must be made on **LOWER** volume than the first, proving that supply has dried up and the path of least resistance is now up.

### 5. Morning Star
**Traditional Definition:** A multi-bar reversal showing a slowdown in downward momentum followed by a sharp push up.

**VSA Correlation:** **Stopping Volume** followed by a **Test** or strong demand.

<Diagram name="candlestick-morning-star" />

- **Verification:** A true bottoming process will often show a narrow spread down-bar with either very high volume (Stopping Volume—buyers stepping in to brake the fall) or very low volume (Falling Pressure).
- **Follow-through:** The third (bullish) bar must push up on increasing (but not climactic) volume to confirm that the path of least resistance is now upwards.
